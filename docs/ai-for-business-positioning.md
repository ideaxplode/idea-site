# AI for Business: Small and Medium Business Positioning

Research date: 2026-10-21

## Correction to the first direction

The first AI transformation concept borrowed useful principles from major consultancies, but its vocabulary and presentation still assumed an enterprise buyer. Terms such as transformation, readiness, governance, value mapping, pilots, and operating models make sense to an internal innovation or IT team. They are less likely to connect with an owner who experiences the problem as missed enquiries, repeated administration, slow customer responses, scattered information, or a lack of timely visibility.

The second concept changes the unit of communication from the AI programme to the owner’s working day.

## What the small-business market communicates

Smaller AI consultancies consistently use more immediate promises:

- [Bushin Consulting](https://www.bushinconsulting.com/) focuses on reducing friction in daily work and creating capacity without adding headcount.
- [Patwon](https://www.patwon.com/) qualifies its audience directly, promises a small number of recommended uses, and stresses one accountable person rather than a junior-team handoff.
- [Ops Garage AI](https://opsgarageai.com/ai-workflow-automation/) describes familiar operational failures such as intake, follow-up, approvals, handoffs, and information copied between ordinary business tools.
- [Move37](https://move37labs.com/) combines practical strategy, workflow automation, custom implementation, and team training for small and mid-sized businesses.

The strongest pages describe the symptom before the technology. They speak about hours, follow-ups, paperwork, repeated tasks, and the burden on a small team. They also make the first commitment feel bounded.

## Wider evidence about SME adoption

The [OECD’s 2025 SME AI adoption report](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-adoption-by-small-and-medium-sized-enterprises_426399c1-en.html) finds that SMEs vary significantly by size, sector, digital maturity, and stage of adoption. It highlights skills, finance, infrastructure, and access to suitable support as important enablers. This argues against a single maturity model or a broad transformation programme as the default entry point.

The [U.S. Chamber of Commerce 2025 small-business technology report](https://www.uschamber.com/technology/empowering-small-business-the-impact-of-technology-on-u-s-small-business) says small-business AI adoption is growing, with prominent uses around customer engagement and inventory management. The important positioning lesson is not the adoption statistic itself. It is that small firms encounter AI through everyday business activities, not through a central innovation function.

## Revised audience

The intended visitor is an owner or senior operator in a small or medium-sized business who:

- is responsible for the result as well as the decision;
- sees operational pain but may not know which technology applies;
- cannot spare a large internal team for discovery or implementation;
- values a modest first win more than a comprehensive strategy document;
- needs direct access to experienced people;
- wants costs, risk, and disruption kept proportionate to the opportunity.

The visitor does not need to identify as an “SME,” understand AI terminology, or arrive with a use case.

## Revised message

**Where does your business lose time, customers, or money? That is where AI should start.**

The page should help the visitor recognise a sentence they already say:

- “We keep doing the same admin again and again.”
- “Some enquiries never get a proper follow-up.”
- “Customers wait while we look for the answer.”
- “Our information is everywhere.”
- “I get the numbers after I needed them.”

Each sentence opens into a plain-language explanation of what AI could do and a sensible first move. The examples remain cross-industry by describing patterns of work rather than sector-specific use cases.

## Revised presentation concept

The `/ai-for-business/` page is styled as an annotated working day rather than a consulting framework. Its hero shows ordinary loose ends accumulating through a day. The problem finder starts with familiar frustrations. The “Read, Write, Remember, Move, Notice, Suggest” section explains AI through practical abilities instead of product categories.

The page makes the three-person team an advantage:

- direct conversation with the people doing the work;
- no sales-to-delivery handoff;
- a small first scope;
- 20+ years of implementation judgment behind the recommendation.

The final call to action asks for one repeating pain point, not a project brief.
